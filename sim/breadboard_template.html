Clock Breadboard Layout

Four-digit test build on two full-size breadboards: two DC56-11 duals, four 74HCT595s, the STM32G030F6 on a TSSOP-20 adapter, an ATGM336H GPS breakout and a 3.3 V regulator. {{STATS}}. Every hole was checked against the circuit by the generator script.

Build order

  1. Check both boards' power rails: on many full-size boards each rail is split in the middle. If yours are, bridge each rail across the split with a short wire.
  2. Board B power first: regulator, its two 10 µF capacitors (stripe = minus, to GND), the ST-Link 5 V/GND wires. Measure 3.3 V on the top rail before going on.
  3. Solder the STM32G030F6 to the adapter, plug it in, add its 100 nF capacitors, then the SWD wires. Flash a blink test to the status LED.
  4. GPS breakout and its three signal wires. Watch NMEA and PPS on the logic analyser (probe points are marked in pink).
  5. Board A: the four 74HCT595s (notch to the left), their power wires and 100 nF capacitors, then the chain wires between chips.
  6. Segment resistors and jumpers, one digit at a time (use Highlight to show one digit). Plug the displays in last, decimal points at the bottom right.
  7. The four wires between the boards: +5 V, GND, data, clock, latch, output enable.

Leads cross over each other in places; that's fine as long as bare leads don't touch. Resistor leads up to about 3.5 cm are needed for the longest runs.

Things to know

Segment order differs from the PCB. To keep resistor runs short, each '595 output drives a different segment here than on the schematic. {{SEGMAP}}

GPS breakout pins: drawn as VCC, GND, TX, RX, PPS. Your breakout's order will likely differ; follow the labels printed on it. It's powered from 5 V; its signal pins are 3.3 V.

TSSOP adapter width: drawn as a 0.6″ wide DIP-20. If yours is 0.3″ wide it sits across the centre gap like a '595; each pin still goes in the same column.

Buttons: drawn with two legs. On a four-leg tactile switch use two diagonally opposite legs.

Power: the ST-Link's 5 V pin is enough for four digits, the GPS and the MCU (about 0.3 A worst case).
