# Schematic notes (rev 0.1)

The schematic is in `hardware/gpsclock/` (KiCad 7 project), with a PDF export
in `hardware/gpsclock/gpsclock.pdf`. It's generated by
`hardware/tools/gen_schematic.py`, so change the script rather than the
`.kicad_sch` file, or the next run overwrites your edits. If you'd rather edit
in KiCad from now on, just stop using the script.

How the sheet is drawn: every pin has a short wire stub ending in a net label
or a power symbol. Pins with the same label are connected. There are no long
wires to follow.

## Checks done

- KiCad 7 parses the file and exports a netlist: 142 parts, 195 nets.
- `hardware/tools/check_netlist.py` compares KiCad's computed connectivity with
  what the script intended. It flags labels that miss a pin, typo'd nets, nets
  with a single pin, inputs with no driver, and two outputs on one net. It
  passes. I also broke a copy on purpose (renamed one label, moved another off
  its pin) to make sure it catches mistakes; it flagged both.
- **Not done:** KiCad's own ERC. The `kicad-cli` that was installable here is
  KiCad 7, which has no ERC command, and the KiCad 8 package source was blocked
  by the network. Open the project in KiCad 7/8 and run Inspect → ERC once.

To regenerate and check:

```
python3 hardware/tools/gen_schematic.py
kicad-cli sch export netlist -o /tmp/gpsclock.net hardware/gpsclock/gpsclock.kicad_sch
python3 hardware/tools/check_netlist.py /tmp/gpsclock.net
kicad-cli sch export pdf -o hardware/gpsclock/gpsclock.pdf hardware/gpsclock/gpsclock.kicad_sch
```

Several G030 TSSOP-20 pins are bonded to more than one GPIO (pins 1, 2, 15,
16, 17, 19, 20). Only one GPIO per pin may be an output, otherwise they fight.
This is the main firmware gotcha with this package.

## Display

- U11–U19 (74HCT595, SOIC-16) drive DS1–DS9 in order: H tens, H units, M tens,
  M units, S tens, S units, 1/10, 1/100, 1/1000 s.
- Chain: SR_DATA → U11 → U12 → … → U19. U19's QH' is unused. The byte for DS9
  goes out first.
- SRCLK, RCLK and /OE are common to all nine. /SRCLR is tied to +5V.
- QA–QG → segments A–G, QH → DP, each through its own 470 Ω (Rn1–Rn8 for digit
  n). That's about 5.7 mA per segment, assuming ~2.0 V red LEDs and ~0.3 V
  output drop, or ≤ 46 mA per '595 with all segments on.
- **Colons:** QH of U12 drives the two LEDs between DS2 and DS3 (D1, D2 via R28,
  R29), and QH of U14 drives the two between DS4 and DS5 (D3, D4 via R48, R49).
  DS2 and DS4's own DP pins are left unconnected. DS6's DP is the decimal point
  before the milliseconds.
- **Digit pinout:** the custom symbol uses the common "5161AS" common-cathode
  pinout (1 E, 2 D, 3 CC, 4 C, 5 DP, 6 B, 7 A, 8 CC, 9 F, 10 G). I confirmed it
  in two places, but I couldn't open a manufacturer datasheet from here.
  **Check it against the datasheet of the exact part you buy** (or with a
  multimeter's diode mode). Some clones swap pins, and the footprint wouldn't
  catch that.
- Footprint: `Display_7Segment:7SegmentLED_LTS6760_LTS6780`, which is
  2 × 5 pins at 2.54 mm pitch with rows 15.24 mm apart. That's the usual 0.56"
  single-digit layout; check the body outline against your part.

## Clock

- Y1 is a 16 MHz 3.3 V CMOS oscillator in a 3225 4-pad package (Abracon ASE
  footprint; most cheap 3225 oscillators share its pinout: 1 EN, 2 GND, 3 OUT,
  4 VDD). Check this against the part you pick.
- Pin 1 is pulled up through R3 (10 k), so EN parts run, NC parts ignore it,
  and the board will also take a CMOS-output TCXO. **Avoid TCXOs whose pin 1
  is VC (voltage control):** R3 would pin VC to the rail.
- R4 (22 Ω) is a series resistor at the oscillator output to soften edges. It
  can be 0 Ω.

## GPS

- U3 ATGM336H-5N31 on the `RF_GPS:ublox_MAX` footprint. The ATGM336H pinout
  matches the u-blox MAX layout pin for pin (GND 1/10/12, TXD 2, RXD 3, PPS 4,
  VBAT 6, VCC 8, RESET 9, RF_IN 11, VCC_RF 14, SDA/SCL 16/17). I matched the
  pins but haven't compared pad dimensions with the ATGM datasheet drawing.
- VBAT (pin 6) is tied to VCC. There's no backup supply, so every power-up is
  a cold start (decided in the analysis).
- ON/OFF (pin 5) is pulled high by R6 (active low). RESET is pulled high by R7
  and the MCU can pull it low.
- **Active antenna feed:** VCC_RF → R8 10 Ω → L1 47 nH → RF_IN line. These are
  typical values for this kind of module, **not checked against the ATGM336H
  reference design**. I couldn't open the datasheet PDF from this environment.
  Check before ordering. If the datasheet says RF_IN isn't DC-blocked inside
  the module, the circuit needs changing.
- RF trace from U3 pin 11 to J2: keep it short, 50 Ω coplanar waveguide, with
  ground stitching. That's a layout task.
- TP1 on PPS, TP2 on SR_LATCH: put a scope on both to see the latch-to-PPS
  timing directly.

## Power

- J1 is a 6-pin power-only USB-C receptacle (GCT USB4125 footprint). R1/R2
  5.1 kΩ on CC1/CC2.
- F1 is a 500 mA hold polyfuse, then +5V.
- U2 AP2112K-3.3 makes +3V3 (MCU, GPS, oscillator; ~40–60 mA). C2 1 µF in,
  C3 10 µF out.
- The '595s and LEDs run straight from +5V. C20/C21 10 µF bulk, plus 100 nF
  per '595 (C11–C19).
- No TVS/ESD on VBUS. Optional; add one if the clock will be plugged and
  unplugged a lot.

## Optional parts (fit or leave off)

- Q1 phototransistor + R10 + C10 on PA0 (auto-brightness). Part not chosen yet,
  so it has no footprint.
- SW1 button, D5/R9 status LED, J4 debug UART: cheap and handy for bring-up.

## Open before PCB layout

1. Check the 7-segment pinout against the purchased part.
2. Check the ATGM336H active-antenna circuit and pad sizes against its
   datasheet.
3. Pick the oscillator part (and TCXO option) and check its pin 1 function.
4. Pick the phototransistor (or drop it).
5. Run KiCad's ERC once in the GUI.
6. Ideally, check items 1 and 2 on a breadboard first: an ATGM336H breakout
   plus one digit and one 74HCT595.
